## How many people does a Frankfurt data centre programme need?

Exyte gave a rare answer in June 2026. Its three new data centre projects in the Greater Frankfurt region, [worth close to €750 million and around 80 MW of IT load, will support roughly 400 roles in Germany](https://www.exyte.net/Newsroom/Exyte-secures-three-major-AI-driven-data-center-projects-near-Frankfurt). All three run in parallel, some inside live operating facilities, and all are due for completion by 2028.

Headcount rarely makes the announcement. Capacity reports talk in megawatts, contract awards talk in euros, and the number of engineers a build will need rarely gets a mention. That makes Exyte's figure useful well beyond Exyte. If you run a programme anywhere in the region, it's the closest thing you'll get to a measure of what a build this size pulls out of the market.

## Where are those 400 people coming from?

Exyte split the number itself. Around 150 roles will be filled by moving experienced staff across from its existing project portfolio, including teams switching between project phases. The other 250 or so will come through external hiring, across engineering and technical roles, project controls, construction and commissioning.

The 250 is the obvious headline, since that's how many people one contractor has to find from outside its own walls, in a single region. The 150 deserves as much attention. Those engineers are moving off other work, and the phases they leave behind still have to be finished by somebody, most likely someone drawn from the same pool of experienced people.

## Why is Frankfurt's hiring pressure building?

Construction isn't slowing down. [CBRE's Q1 2026 figures put Frankfurt's data centre supply up 23% year on year](https://www.cbre.co.uk/press-releases/european-data-centre-supply-surges-as-growth-expands-beyond-core-markets-finds-cbre-research), the fastest growth of Europe's four largest markets, with 218 MW of net absorption. Vacancy across Frankfurt's data centre space stood at 5%, the lowest of the four.

That vacancy figure measures floor space, and it still matters for hiring. When almost every megawatt is let as soon as it's built, the pressure to build more doesn't ease, and each new hall needs the same electrical, mechanical and commissioning skills as the last one. CBRE also reports that development is spreading out of the city to sites where land and power are easier to secure. Same workforce, wider map.

## How long do these projects hold on to their engineers?

Longer than the announcements suggest. [CyrusOne announced its FRA7 campus at Frankfurt Westside in September 2023](https://www.cyrusone.com/resources/press-releases/cyrusone-announces-new-frankfurt-data-center-with-beos-and-swiss-life-asset-managers), with 81 MW across nine data halls in two buildings, construction planned from Q3 2024 and capacity due by Q2 2026. CyrusOne's website still lists the site as in development.

Large builds run long, and nobody should read that as a failing. What it shows is that a project announced three years ago can still be holding its engineers today, while new awards like Exyte's land on top. The people already on site don't come free when the next contract is signed, and every programme in the region is fishing in the same water.

## What do these engineers cost in Frankfurt?

Isaac Vassell, Rectify's founder, has personally placed engineers into Frankfurt's data centre sector at the salaries below. They are individual placements, not market averages, and they show how far apart roles on the same kind of programme can sit.

| Senior Mechanical Engineer (HLKS) | 9 years | €95,000 |

| Electrical Construction Manager | 5 years | €65,000 |

Founder-placed by Isaac Vassell, Frankfurt data centre sector. Role, city and figure only.

The two Senior Mechanical Engineers went into the same Frankfurt company, with the same job title, €10,000 apart. A programme that prices its hires off job titles, or off a quick look at adverts, can be out by that much before the search even starts. With around 250 external hires heading into one region from one contractor alone, those mistakes add up quickly.

## How Rectify reads this

We read every major project announcement in Frankfurt as a hiring event. A contract award tells you the date a programme starts competing for people, and Exyte's 400 is a rare look at how big that competition gets. The figure most announcements leave out is the one that decides whether the 2028 completion date holds.

The delivery directors who come through this well are the ones who plan their critical hires from the day the contract is signed. By the time the next award lands, the engineers they need may already have a better offer down the road.

## What should delivery directors hiring in Frankfurt do?

Treat every major award in the region, your own or a competitor's, as the signal to lock in critical roles. Plan headcount from the contract date, brief each role on the phases and scope it actually needs, since the title alone won't price it, and assume the engineers you want are already committed somewhere else.

The people who matter most on a data centre build, the commissioning leads, authorised persons and senior mechanical and electrical engineers, are rarely looking for work. Reaching them means knowing which programmes they're on and when those programmes will let them go. If you're planning Frankfurt hires for 2027 or 2028 and want a read on where the pressure sits for your roles, that conversation is worth having before your competitors have it.
