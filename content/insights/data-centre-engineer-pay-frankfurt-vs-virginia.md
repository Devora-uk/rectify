Rectify International is a specialist recruitment firm placing MEP, building-services engineering and data centre talent, with a primary focus on Germany and the United States. This analysis is our own, worked from official pay, tax and rent figures across four countries. Nobody else has published these numbers.

## How much does a data centre engineer keep after tax and rent?

A median electrical engineer in the Washington DC metro area ends the year with about €57,900 after income tax, social contributions and a one-bed rent. The same engineer keeps €40,600 in Frankfurt, €35,500 in Helsinki and €28,800 in London (derived, Rectify calculation from official data).

London's place at the bottom is the surprise. It has the lowest tax take of the four, and it still comes last, because rent swallows almost half of what lands in the bank. Frankfurt has the highest tax take, and it comes second, because rent there is the cheapest of the four by some distance. The gap that matters for a German employer sits at the top of the table, and it is wide. An engineer in Northern Virginia keeps about €17,300 a year more than one in Frankfurt.

## Why is everyone hiring at once?

Data centre hiring in Europe has climbed well above where it was before the pandemic, while the rest of tech hiring has not. Indeed's data, reported by Euronews in October 2026, shows data centre job postings up 25% on pre-pandemic levels in Germany and 24% in the UK by August 2026. In nine of the ten European countries Indeed analysed, other tech postings were still below their pre-pandemic level.

Employers are already paying to compete. Data centre roles advertised higher pay than the same occupation in other sectors in 28 of the 29 country and occupation pairs Indeed checked. In Germany, installation and maintenance roles at data centres advertised 36% more than the same jobs elsewhere. The buyer side looks very different on each side of the Atlantic, too. In the US, ten big tech firms account for 71% of data centre postings, while in Europe it takes more than 500 firms to reach 70%. A German contractor is competing with a crowd for engineers, and with a handful of very deep pockets in the US.

## Germany takes the biggest slice of pay, so why does Frankfurt still beat London?

Germany takes more of an engineer's salary than any of the other three countries, and cheaper housing hands most of it back. At the median, 39.9% of a Frankfurt engineer's gross pay goes on income tax and social contributions, against 24.5% in London, 27.8% around Washington DC and 31.9% in Helsinki (derived).

The OECD's own figures put the countries in the same order. Its Taxing Wages 2026 report shows a single worker on 167% of the average wage losing 42.3% of pay to income tax and employee contributions in Germany, 38.8% in Finland, 30.7% in the UK and 29.2% in the US.

The make-up of that German deduction is worth a look. Our Frankfurt engineer pays slightly more into social insurance (about €16,200 for pension, health, care and unemployment cover) than in income tax (about €15,600). A London engineer on a similar salary pays about £3,200 in National Insurance. Part of what a German employee loses at source comes back as health and pension cover that an American employee often pays for separately, which matters when a candidate compares two offers line by line.

Then rent does the rest. Frankfurt's 2026 rent survey puts the average net cold rent at €12.28 per square metre, so a 50 square metre flat costs about €614 a month. A one-bed in London averaged £1,760 in August 2026. Rent takes 15% of a Frankfurt engineer's take-home pay and 46% of a London engineer's (derived).

## What would Frankfurt have to pay to match Northern Virginia?

To leave an engineer with the same money after tax and rent as the median electrical engineer in the Washington DC metro area, a Frankfurt employer would need to pay about €113,900 a year (derived). The Frankfurt median we used is €79,800, so that is a gap of roughly €34,000.

The figure moves with the rent you assume. An engineer arriving in Frankfurt today pays the asking rent on a new let, which immowelt put at €17.12 per square metre in October 2026. On that basis the matching salary rises to about €119,300. Our working range is €114,000 to €120,000, and we'd quote it as a range.

Against London and Helsinki the picture is much kinder to Frankfurt. A Frankfurt salary of about €57,400 matches what the median London engineer keeps, and about €70,500 matches Helsinki (derived). Both sit below the Frankfurt median. Even at Frankfurt asking rents, the median Frankfurt engineer keeps about €37,700, still ahead of Helsinki and London.

## Does the picture hold if everyone earns the same?

Yes for tax, and the order shifts slightly for take-home. Put the same €100,000 gross salary into all four systems and Germany still takes the most, 42.0%, against 37.2% in Finland, 29.5% in the UK and 26.8% in the US (derived).

On a level salary, the finishing order after rent is Helsinki first (€51,400), then Frankfurt (€50,600), Washington DC (€49,600) and London (€45,500). So Northern Virginia's lead comes mainly from higher pay for the same job, and only partly from lower tax. A German employer can't change the tax code, so the lever it controls is the offer.

## What this means if you're hiring data centre engineers in Germany

A Frankfurt electrical or commissioning engineer with a good CV can weigh an offer from London, Helsinki or Virginia alongside one from the contractor down the road. On take-home pay, Frankfurt holds its own against London and Helsinki, and the US is the offer that turns a hesitant candidate into a lost one. Plan the package around the after-rent number, because that's the sum a mobile candidate works out at the kitchen table.

Three practical points follow. Show candidates the after-rent figure, because Frankfurt wins that comparison against London and Helsinki and loses only to the US. Budget senior and hard-to-fill roles against the US benchmark, where the gap is real. Move quickly once you find the right person, since a candidate with a US offer on the table is unlikely to wait for a slow process.

## Method and caveats

We compared median gross pay for electrical engineers, the closest official match for data centre engineering roles, in four hubs. Sources were BLS for the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area (May 2025), ONS ASHE for the UK (April 2025, full-time), the Hessian State Statistics Office for expert-level electrical engineers in Hessen (April 2025) and the TEK labour market survey for Finland (October 2024). We applied 2026 income tax and employee social contribution rules for a single employee with no children and no church tax, including Virginia state tax and Helsinki municipal tax. We took off 12 months of rent for a one-bed home: HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for the Washington metro area, the ONS one-bed average for London, Statistics Finland's median two-room rent for Helsinki, and the Frankfurt 2026 rent survey average applied to a 50 square metre flat. Figures were converted at ECB reference rates of 8 October 2026.

There are limits. The German figure excludes holiday pay and bonuses, so it likely understates annual pay. The UK figure is national, not London-specific. The Finnish figure covers all academic engineers. Rent sources measure slightly different things (the US figure includes utilities, the Frankfurt figure doesn't). We net off rent only, not food, transport or healthcare, and US workers usually pay part of their health insurance on top. Every derived figure is labelled as such, and the full calculation workbook is available on request.
